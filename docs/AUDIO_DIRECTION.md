# Audio direction

Sound is part of the core knockback interaction. Established direction: playful competition, layered impacts, controlled variation and adaptive intensity.

New starting direction: buoyant percussion, playful synth/plucked melody and warm low impact body. Avoid oppressive cinematic combat or one fatiguing nonstop loop. Exact tracks, instrumentation and tempo are creative exploration; initial music sketch target 110–130 BPM.

Sound events: dash, weak/strong impact, major knockback, elimination, hazard warning, ability, countdown, victory/defeat, reward and UI confirmation. An impact combines transient/body/whoosh/character reaction as appropriate. Use multiple variants and subtle seeded presentation variation; audio randomness never enters simulation. Starting variation examples: pitch ±5%, gain ±3%, adjustable after listening.

Music stems share tempo and bar structure: lobby, active-round base, escalating hazard/final-survivor layer, sudden-death intensity and outcome sting. Use authoritative round events to request musical transitions. Historical 0:45/1:30 cues were examples; do not hardcode them across all modes.

Prioritize warning → immediate impact → character feedback → music/ambience. Limit simultaneous voices and duck supporting audio rather than stacking every layer at full level. Independent master/music/SFX controls, mute persistence and browser audio unlock are required. No essential information is sound-only.

Keep editable masters and rights records, trim silence, apply fades, test seamless loops and export compatible compressed runtime audio after browser checks. Listen through headphones and ordinary speakers at crowded-match density. Reject clicks, clipping, repeated identical hits and music masking telegraphs. M3 evidence includes an actual listened-to recording and mix notes.
