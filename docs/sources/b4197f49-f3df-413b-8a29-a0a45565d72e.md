## agentMessage 0e53cd16-7deb-4118-8a2c-623f8ed6dd2f

Yes — I think there is a bigger opportunity here than just “Push Arena.”

The thing I would preserve is not the exact **last-player-standing mode**. I would preserve the **core interaction model**:

**move + dash + bump/push + use something + interact with the environment**

Then almost everything else becomes a ruleset layered on top of the same engine.

That gives you something closer to a **party-action engine** than a single game.

## The shared foundation

I would make the engine understand a few universal concepts:

**Players**
have movement, dash, knockback, inventory slots, abilities, teams, status effects, respawn state.

**Objects**
can be carried, thrown, activated, defended, collected, pushed, broken, captured.

**Hazards**
can push, damage, stun, slow, launch, eliminate, or change terrain.

**Objectives**
can be survival, score, capture, escort, collect, defeat, defend, race, or cooperate.

**Rules**
control things such as:
- round time
- lives
- teams
- bot count
- weapon/item availability
- ability availability
- hazard frequency
- respawns
- knockback strength
- score target
- map mutations

Once that exists, you can build a lot of games without rebuilding the networking and movement layer.

# The game modes I think fit best

| Mode | Basic idea | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Push Arena** | Last player/team alive | Purest version of your core mechanic |
| **King of the Hill** | Hold a moving or fixed zone | Forces players to collide instead of hiding |
| **Crown Chase** | One player holds the crown; others try to knock it away | Very easy to understand and funny |
| **Boss Battle** | Everyone works together against one giant boss | Gives you the cooperative experience you're interested in |
| **Team Knockout** | 3v3 / 4v4 with limited lives | More tactical than free-for-all |
| **Treasure Grab** | Collect objects and return them to your team's base | Adds carrying, stealing, defending |
| **Hot Potato** | Pass or knock an unstable object into someone else before time expires | Great party-game chaos |
| **Payload Push** | Teams physically push/escort an object toward a goal | Your push mechanic becomes the objective itself |
| **Relic Hold** | Carry an object for as long as possible while everyone hunts you | Like King of the Hill but mobile |
| **Survival Waves** | Players cooperate against bots, traps, creatures, or hazards | Strong local/co-op mode |
| **Boss Rush** | Short sequence of increasingly difficult bosses | Strong replayable cooperative mode |
| **Obstacle Race** | Race through hazards while still being allowed to push others | Reuses movement/map systems |
| **Infection** | One or two players begin infected; knocked-out players switch teams | Naturally escalates toward chaos |
| **Bounty Hunt** | Each player has a target, but somebody else is hunting them | Encourages movement and unpredictability |
| **Zone Collapse** | Teams must continually move between safe zones | Combines survival with objective play |
| **Capture the Creature** | Grab a roaming NPC/object and keep it away from everybody else | Silly and highly readable |
| **Protect the VIP** | One player or bot must survive while teammates protect them | Cooperative/team-focused |
| **Raid Escape** | Complete 2–3 objectives together and escape before time expires | Closest to the social/co-op feel you described |

The last one is interesting because it starts moving toward the **Gorilla Tag / Scary Baboon style “we're together doing stuff” experience**, while still giving you a beginning, objective, and ending.

# I think you want three families of play

Rather than making 20 unrelated modes, I'd consciously divide the engine into three categories.

### Competitive

This is the original Push Arena identity.

Examples:

**Push Arena**
**King of the Hill**
**Crown Chase**
**Team Knockout**
**Hot Potato**
**Treasure Grab**

Matches generally last **2–5 minutes**.

### Cooperative

This gets you closer to the social games you're attracted to.

Examples:

**Boss Battle**
**Survival Waves**
**Raid Escape**
**Protect the VIP**
**Boss Rush**

These could be more like **5–10 minute sessions** rather than 2-minute rounds.

That doesn't mean they need to become endless open-world games.

You can still have:

> enter lobby → choose mission → complete mission → rewards → return to lobby

That gives you cooperative hanging-out without eliminating structure.

### Party / Chaos

These are more experimental.

Examples:

**Race**
**Floor Is Lava**
**Random Mutators**
**Hot Potato**
**Infection**
**Low Gravity**
**Everyone Has Grapples**
**Super Knockback**

These could be excellent for private rooms.

# Weapons and items could work — but I would change how you think about them

I would be careful about turning Push Arena into:

> guns + health bars + damage + ammo + weapon rarity + armor

because suddenly you've made a completely different game.

Instead, make weapons **extensions of pushing and positioning**.

For example:

**Hammer**
Huge knockback, slow swing.

**Boxing Glove**
Quick short-range punch.

**Air Cannon**
Blasts players away.

**Grapple**
Pull yourself or someone else.

**Boomerang**
Hits and briefly stuns.

**Magnet**
Pulls objects/players toward you.

**Spring Mine**
Launches whoever steps on it.

**Snowball**
Small knockback and slippery status.

**Sticky Bomb**
Attaches and explodes with knockback rather than damage.

**Bubble Gun**
Temporarily puts someone in a floating bubble.

**Vacuum**
Sucks nearby players/items toward you.

**Rocket Boots**
Temporary giant dash.

Those still feel like **Push Arena**.

# Items can create a great second layer

You could have map pickups:

```text
Mystery Box
     ↓
random temporary item
```

Maybe players can carry:

**one item + one ability**

That keeps controls extremely simple.

For example:

- Left stick/WASD = move
- Dash
- Ability
- Item
- Emote/interact

That's enough.

Don't give players six inventory slots.

---

# Permanent abilities versus temporary items

I would distinguish them.

An **ability** is part of your loadout.

Examples:

Shockwave  
Shield  
Blink  
Ground Slam  
Magnet  
Grapple

An **item** is something you find during the match.

Examples:

Hammer  
Bomb  
Speed Boost  
Spring Trap  
Air Cannon  
Bubble  
Snowball

That creates variety without overwhelming character builds.

# Boss battles could be especially good

This is one area I'd invest in.

Imagine four Push Arena creatures fighting a huge **Sky Titan**.

The boss isn't primarily about reducing a health bar.

Instead:

```text
Boss slams platform
↓
shockwave pushes players

Boss destroys tiles

Boss summons spinning bumpers

Players activate three switches

Boss becomes vulnerable

Players push energy bombs into boss

Boss gets knocked backward

Next phase begins
```

Now the **Push Arena mechanics are still the mechanics**.

You're just using them cooperatively.

That is important.

You don't want:

> PvP mode = Push Arena mechanics  
> Boss mode = suddenly World of Warcraft combat

Keep the interaction language consistent.

# Imagine a volcano boss

A giant lava creature sits beyond the arena.

It:

- punches platforms
- creates lava waves
- spits fireballs that knock players
- causes tiles to disappear
- launches meteor rocks

Players have to push explosive magma cores into three launchers.

Launchers fire them back into the boss.

That sounds like a completely different mode while using almost the same engine.

**Solo + Bots**

**Couch Co-op Boss Battles**

# Bots become much more valuable than matchmaking filler

Then your CPU system can support:

**Competitive bots**
try to win Arena.

**Objective bots**
understand King of the Hill and Treasure Grab.

**Cooperative bots**
help humans complete boss objectives.

**Party bots**
behave imperfectly and sometimes do funny things.

You can even eventually give them personalities:

**Aggressor**
tries to knock everybody out.

**Survivor**
avoids danger.

**Objective Player**
prioritizes zones/items.

**Troublemaker**
uses environmental hazards.

**Support**
protects teammates.

That makes offline play much less sterile.

# There's also an interesting middle ground between “match” and “endless lobby”

I think this may solve the thing you like about those cooperative games.

Imagine a **social staging area**.

You join a public/random room with maybe 8 players.

You're in a small interactive hub.

Players can:

- emote
- run around
- practice pushing
- inspect cosmetics
- do tiny interactions
- vote/select challenges
- join the next activity

Every few minutes:

> EVENT STARTING

Maybe:

**Sky Titan Attack**

Everyone jumps through the portal.

Five-minute boss fight.

Then everyone comes back.

A few minutes later:

**Crown Chase**

Then:

**Obstacle Run**

Then:

**Treasure Hunt**

People can stay in that room together.

Now you have some of the **persistent social-cooperative feeling** you're describing without throwing away structured game rounds.

I actually think that could become very compelling eventually.

# Monetization fits even better if all modes share an identity

The model you described makes sense:

**Everyone earns currency by playing.**

**Someone who wants cosmetics faster can buy currency.**

But:

> spending money never makes you better at pushing people.

That currency can buy:

- outfits
- hats
- shoes
- trails
- emotes
- victory poses
- elimination effects
- profile cards
- lobby animations
- item skins
- ability visual effects

And importantly:

**the same character/cosmetics can travel across game modes.**

So if I earn a neon outfit in Boss Rush, I can wear it in King of the Hill.

That dramatically increases the perceived value of cosmetics.

# If you create multiple games, I wouldn't split them immediately

I think this part matters.

You've said you're willing to create multiple games from the same engine.

Architecturally, **absolutely do that**.

Product-wise, I wouldn't start with:

```text
Push Arena
Push Boss
Push Racing
Push Quest
Push Party
```

as five different websites/games.

You'll divide your audience five ways.

I'd initially build:

# PUSH ARENA

with multiple modes.

Underneath, internally:

```text
Push Engine
├── Arena
├── Objectives
├── Co-op
├── Racing
├── Bots
├── Items
├── Abilities
└── Maps
```

If one mode becomes unexpectedly huge later — say your boss/co-op experience becomes more popular than Arena — **then** you can spin it into its own game while reusing the engine.

# The core engine should therefore be slightly broader than your current spec

I would modify your architectural goal from:

> multiplayer knockback arena engine

to:

> **multiplayer/local party-action engine built around movement, knockback, interactions, objectives, bots, items, hazards, and configurable rulesets.**

That's not actually dramatically more complicated.

But it changes a few early architecture choices in important ways.

For example, instead of coding:

```text
if lastPlayerAlive:
    endMatch()
```

you build:

```text
GameMode
    initialize()
    update()
    evaluateObjective()
    handlePlayerEvent()
    calculateScore()
    determineWinner()
```

Then:

```text
LastPlayerStandingMode
KingOfHillMode
BossMode
TreasureMode
RaceMode
```

all plug into the same system.

That decision is **much cheaper to make now than later.**

# If I were narrowing the first version

I still would **not** build everything above.

I'd prove four experiences:

### 1. Push Arena
Pure PvP.

Tests whether the basic game is fun.

### 2. King of the Hill
Objective PvP.

Tests whether the engine supports something beyond elimination.

### 3. Boss Battle
Co-op.

Tests whether the same mechanics can create cooperative gameplay.

### 4. Local Party
2–4 local players + configurable bots.

Tests whether Push Arena works as a living-room / offline game.

If those four all feel good, you've proven something much bigger than one game mode.

You've proven the **engine**.

And I suspect your longer-term opportunity may actually be:

> **a configurable social party-action platform where the same cute characters move between competitive, cooperative and chaotic challenges.**

That preserves the simplicity of Push Arena while giving you the broader cooperative/social direction you're drawn to.
